Fractional CMO cost for a small business, against a full-time marketing director.

For a small business, a fractional CMO typically costs €30,000 to €60,000 a year: two to four days a month at a €1,350 day rate. A €90,000 marketing director in France costs about €130,000 once employer charges land, before the search fee and the ramp. The fractional day is more expensive. You just buy far fewer of them.

Fractional CMO cost for a small business starts with the wrong comparison

Most founders compare the fractional CMO day rate with a salary divided by 220. That's the wrong sum. On that basis the fractional option always looks expensive, because it is: €1,350 a day is more than any salaried director costs per day. The real question for a small business is different. How many senior marketing days does the company actually need each month, and what does it pay for every day it doesn't use?

A full-time marketing director is a fixed cost for five days a week, whether the business needs strategic judgment on all five or only on three a month. A fractional engagement prices only the days you use. For a company with ten to fifty people, where the marketing function is still being defined, that gap in utilisation matters far more than the gap in daily rate. The general ranges are covered in how much a fractional CMO costs. This page does the small-business version, line by line.

The fully-loaded cost of a full-time marketing director

Take an illustrative French marketing director on €90,000 gross a year. Pick your own number, the method holds. Employer social contributions for a manager in France run at roughly 45% of gross salary, per URSSAF's published employer contribution rates. That alone turns €90,000 into about €130,500 before the person has opened a laptop.

Then count the days you actually get. French managers on a forfait jours contract work at most 218 days a year, the ceiling set by article L3121-64 of the Code du travail, and five weeks of paid leave are a legal minimum. Divide €130,500 by 218 and each working day costs about €600. That's the honest per-day figure, and yes, it's well below a fractional day rate.

Three costs never appear in that €600. A recruiter's fee if the role goes through a search firm, usually charged as a percentage of first-year salary. The ramp, the months a new hire spends learning the product, the buyers and the politics before output matches cost. If the first three months are mostly learning, year one delivers around 163 productive days, and each one costs closer to €800. And the exit: ending a French CDI is slow and costly when the hire doesn't work out. The small business carries all three risks on a single seat.

The same logic applies outside France. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median wage for marketing managers above $150,000 (Occupational Outlook Handbook, May 2023 data), and its Employer Costs for Employee Compensation survey shows benefits making up roughly 30% of total compensation for private-industry workers. Different taxes, same shape: the salary is the smallest honest number in the file.

What a fractional CMO actually costs a small business

A fractional CMO invoice has two variables: the day rate and the number of days. At Focus4ward the day rate starts at €1,350 excluding tax, and it doesn't move. What moves is the number of days, sized to the problem. There are no employer charges, no paid leave, no search fee and no severance line.

Three shapes cover most small-business engagements. Two to four days a month over eleven working months is 22 to 44 days, or roughly €29,700 to €59,400 a year. That's enough to own the strategy, set priorities, review what the team or agency ships and hold the number. One day a week over 46 weeks is 46 days, about €62,100, for a business actively rebuilding its go-to-market. Two days a week is 92 days, about €124,200, and at that point the comparison with a full-time hire gets genuinely close.

The ramp is shorter too, for a structural reason rather than a heroic one. A senior fractional CMO has run the same diagnostic many times, so the first weeks produce a written read of the business instead of a slow orientation. The case for paying a higher day rate to get that speed is made in full in why the higher day rate costs you less.

When the full-time marketing director wins

The crossover sits around two senior days a week, every week, with no end date. Past that point the fractional premium stops paying for itself, and a permanent hire is the cheaper and stronger answer. Signs you're there: a marketing team of several people who need daily management, channels already proven and now being scaled against a known cost per lead, and a board or leadership team that expects marketing in every weekly decision.

Before that point, a full-time director is usually hired to answer a question the business hasn't asked yet. Which buyer, which message, which channel. A small business that doesn't know those answers is paying €130,500 a year for someone to go and find out, with a CDI attached. The fractional route finds out first, then hires the right full-time profile against a job description that is finally real. The wider argument for that sequencing is in why small businesses choose a fractional CMO over a full-time hire.

One thing stays annoying, whichever route you pick. Neither a salary nor a day rate tells you what the marketing will produce. That's what a marketing audit is for: it turns "what should marketing cost us" into "what should marketing deliver, and how many senior days does that take".

You're not choosing between a salary and a day rate. You're choosing how many senior days your business can actually use, and refusing to pay for the rest.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a fractional CMO cost a small business per year?

It depends on the day rate and the number of days, nothing else. At a day rate of 1,350 EUR excluding tax, two to four days a month over eleven working months comes to roughly 30,000 to 60,000 EUR a year. One day a week over 46 weeks is about 62,000 EUR. There are no employer charges, no paid leave and no search fee on top.

Is a fractional CMO cheaper than a full-time marketing director?

Per day, no. A fractional day rate is higher than the daily cost of a salaried director. Per year, usually yes, because a small business rarely needs five senior days a week. It needs a few days a month of senior judgment and cheaper hands for execution. The comparison only flips once the business genuinely needs two or more senior days every week, indefinitely.

What hidden costs does a full-time marketing hire carry?

Four that never appear in the salary line. Employer social charges, which URSSAF rates put at roughly 45% on top of gross salary for a French manager. Paid leave, which in France is five weeks a year by law. A search fee if the role goes through a recruiter. And ramp time, the months a new hire spends learning the business before their output matches their cost.

When does a full-time marketing director become the better deal?

When the business needs senior marketing attention two or more days every week with no end date, when there is a team large enough to need daily management, and when the channels that produce pipeline are already known. At that point the day-rate premium stops paying for itself and a permanent hire is the cheaper, stronger answer.
